Tankless Water Heater in Covington, KY

A tankless water heater heats water only when a tap opens, using a burner or elements and a heat exchanger. We install, flush and repair them for Covington homes.

How a tankless water heater works and when it makes sense

A tankless water heater, also called an on-demand heater, has no storage tank. When you open a hot tap, flow sensors start the burner or elements, and water passes through a heat exchanger and leaves hot in a few seconds. The DOE puts tankless life at about 20 years, compared with 10 to 15 for a storage tank.

In Covington, KY, the pitch is simple: no 40-gallon reserve to run out of, less floor space, and no tank to burst at midnight. The trade-off is that the unit must deliver a lot of heat at once, so gas line size, venting and electrical capacity decide whether a house suits one.

Cold shower halfway through? Your tank may be too small

If your family empties a 40-gallon tank before the last shower, or the recovery wait is longer than patience allows, the fix is often a different kind of heater and not a bigger tank. Tankless units heat as long as water is flowing, which ends the run-out problem. That is why the next sections look at flow rate, fuel type, venting and care.

Flushing and descaling a tankless heater

Mineral scale is the leading cause of tankless problems. It coats the heat exchanger, narrows the passages, and forces the unit to work harder, which leads to error codes, uneven temperature, noise and early failure. Flushing circulates a mild acid, commonly white vinegar, through the exchanger to dissolve it.

A flush needs isolation valves, which let a plumber close off the house supply and connect a small pump and hose to the service ports. Without them, the job requires adding the valves first. The unit runs the cleaning solution through for around 45 minutes to an hour, then is rinsed with clean water, and the inlet screen is cleaned.

How often to flush depends on your water. We do not quote a hardness number for Northern Kentucky Water District supply. Instead, test your tap with an inexpensive kit and ask NKWD for its annual water quality report. Then follow the maker's guidance, since some manufacturers call for yearly service and others a longer interval.

Why a tankless unit leaks or throws an error

The cause is usually mechanical or scale-related, and the display code tells us where to look.

Scale on the heat exchanger

Reduced flow and overheating trigger codes and can crack the exchanger over time, which then leaks.

Loose or worn connections

Union fittings, flex connectors and isolation valves can seep and leave puddles on the floor beneath the unit.

Condensate drain problems

Condensing gas models make acidic condensate that must drain freely. A blocked line backs up and drips from the cabinet.

Vent faults

Blocked, disconnected or sagging vent pipe causes ignition errors and shutdowns, and exhaust leaks are a safety problem.

Freeze damage

A unit on an exterior wall or in an unheated garage can crack in a cold snap if power or protection is lost.

Venting basics for gas tankless units

Gas tankless units move a lot of hot exhaust through small-diameter pipe, so vent material, length and slope matter. Condensing models run cooler and can use plastic pipe, while non-condensing models need metal vent rated for the temperature. Many run direct vent, pulling air in from outside.

If you are swapping a gas tank, the existing chimney or flue rarely suits the new unit as it is. Expect a new sidewall or roof vent run, and know that a gas leak or exhaust smell is a reason to leave the building and call your gas utility or 911, not a plumber.

What a tankless install or conversion involves

A conversion from tank to tankless is bigger than a like-for-like swap.

  1. 1

    Load check

    We add up the fixtures you run at once and compare that flow and temperature rise to what the unit can deliver.

  2. 2

    Fuel and vent plan

    Gas line size, regulator and vent route are checked, or the electrical panel and wire run for an electric unit.

  3. 3

    Mount and connect

    The unit goes on the wall with isolation valves on both water lines and a relief or condensate drain path.

  4. 4

    Test and teach

    We check temperature at the farthest tap, review error codes, and show you the flush ports.
