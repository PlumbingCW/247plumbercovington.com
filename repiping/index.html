Repiping in Covington, KY

Repiping is replacing a home's worn supply pipes with new copper or PEX. Licensed plumbers plan repipes for older Covington, KY houses and give a clear price before work starts.

Licensed and insuredClear price before work startsSame-day appointments when availableWhole-house or partial

What repiping actually replaces

Repiping replaces the water supply lines inside a house, the pipes that bring pressure to sinks, tubs, toilets and appliances. Drain pipes are a separate system. In an older Covington, KY home, the supply pipes may be galvanized steel, early copper or a plastic that was later found to fail.

A repipe is planned work. You decide when the water goes off, which walls get opened and what the new pipe will be. The alternative is waiting for the pipe to decide for you.

Rusty water or weak pressure in the shower?

Brown or orange water, a shower that dribbles while the sink runs fine, or the third pinhole leak this year all point to pipe that is failing from the inside. The technical name for fixing it is repiping, and it starts with finding out whether the problem is one stretch or the whole house.

Signs the pipes are done

One sign is a repair. Several is a repipe conversation.

Brown or orange water

Rust flaking inside galvanized pipe, especially after the water has sat overnight.

Pressure drops everywhere

Pipe walls build up scale until a half-inch line carries a fraction of its original flow.

Pinhole leaks

Tiny sprays in copper or a damp spot on drywall that keeps coming back in new places.

Green crust at joints

Copper corroding at fittings. A single patch is fine. A pattern is not.

Stains on ceilings

A wet ring below a bathroom or hallway can mean a leaking supply line above. A leak detection visit finds the exact spot.

Whole house or just the bad section?

Partial repiping makes sense when the damage is limited, such as one bathroom branch or the stretch of pipe between the meter and the water heater. A short run is cheaper and less disruptive. If the pipe is the same age and material throughout, though, the next failure is usually only a matter of time.

Whole-house repiping replaces everything from the main to each fixture in one project. It costs more at once but ends the repair cycle. Failing service pipe from the meter into the house is a separate job, covered on the water line repair page.

Copper or PEX

Copper lasts for decades, resists heat and is familiar to every plumber. It costs more in material and takes more labor. PEX is flexible plastic tubing that threads through walls with fewer fittings, tolerates freezing better than rigid pipe and often means smaller holes in the drywall.

Neither is perfect. Copper can pit in some water, and PEX has to be protected from sunlight and sharp edges. A plumber who looks at your walls, basement and crawl access can say which works better in your house.

How a repipe is planned and done

A typical order of work.

  1. 1

    Inspect and map

    The plumber traces every supply line, checks pressure and decides where the new pipe can run.

  2. 2

    Price and permit

    You get a clear price before work starts. Permit rules vary, so ask the Kentucky Division of Plumbing or the local plumbing inspector.

  3. 3

    Replace in stages

    Walls and ceilings are opened where needed, and new pipe is pressure-tested before anything is covered.

  4. 4

    Close up and flush

    Water is restored, lines are flushed, fixtures are reconnected and the work is inspected where required.

Older Covington housing and what is behind the walls

Covington's population peaked at 65,252 in 1930, and Mainstrasse Village alone is a 91-acre historic district of Federal, Victorian and Italianate buildings. Houses from that era were piped in galvanized steel or early copper, so any that have not been repiped may be due. A home in a historic district may also need extra care opening plaster walls.

The Kentucky Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction says new installations and moving fixtures need a plumbing permit, and work inside walls must be inspected before it is covered. Permit rules vary, so ask the Division of Plumbing or the local plumbing inspector early in the plan. Related planned work is on our plumbing installation page.
