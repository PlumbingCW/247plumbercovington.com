Toilet repair means fixing the parts that fill, flush and seal a toilet: the fill valve, flapper, wax ring, supply line and mounting bolts. In Covington, KY the cause is usually found in the first few minutes, just by watching where the water actually goes.

What the toilet is telling you

Each symptom points to a short list of parts.

Runs after the flush

A flapper that does not seat, a chain that is too long, or a fill valve that never shuts off. Hissing at night is the same story.

Refills by itself

The tank drops a little every few minutes and the fill valve tops it up. The flapper is leaking into the bowl.

Water around the base

A failed wax ring, a loose closet bolt, or a sweating tank can all put water on the floor. Wet at the supply line points to the connector instead.

Rocks or shifts

Loose bolts, an uneven floor or a flange that sits below the tile. Rocking breaks the wax seal a little more with every use.

Bowl rises, then drains slowly

A clog in the trap or the drain behind it. A plunger fixes some, a closet auger fixes more, and a deeper blockage needs the line cleared.

The five-minute dye test

The EPA suggests adding a few drops of food coloring to the tank and waiting without flushing. If color shows up in the bowl within 10 minutes, the flapper is leaking. That silent leak is how a toilet quietly adds to the 9,300 gallons a year the EPA says the average home wastes.

Wax rings, flanges and rocking toilets

A wax ring is a thick gasket sitting between the toilet horn and the closet flange, the pipe fitting screwed to the floor. It seals sewer gas and wastewater. Once a toilet has rocked or the ring has dried out, water seeps out at the base and slowly rots the subfloor.

Fixing it means turning off the supply, draining the tank, lifting the toilet and inspecting the flange before anything goes back down. A flange that is cracked, rusted or sitting below the finished floor needs a repair ring or replacement, or the new wax will fail the same way.

Covington's population peaked at 65,252 in 1930, and the city still has 20,576 housing units, so plenty of bathrooms here date back decades. Old closet flanges can be cast iron or lead, and either one can corrode or crack when a toilet is pulled. If your home is that old, expect the plumber to inspect and possibly replace the flange, not just swap the wax.

Moving a toilet to a new spot counts as moving a fixture. When a new toilet makes more sense

Cracked china, a failing tank and repeated wax ring jobs all point toward replacement on an old fixture. A new toilet, set on a sound flange, ends the guessing about where the next leak is coming from.

If you are swapping a toilet or adding a bathroom, our new fixture installation work covers setting the flange, supply and drain. A toilet that clogs in every bathroom is a drain problem, so a drain cleaning visit may solve what a new toilet cannot.
