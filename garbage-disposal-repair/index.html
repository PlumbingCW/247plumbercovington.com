Disposal humming, leaking or backing up tonight

A disposal that is leaking into the cabinet or pushing sink water up into the dishwasher is a mess you do not want to leave until morning, and our 24/7 emergency line is open at any hour. Switch the wall switch off, then turn off the breaker so the motor cannot start. Put a bowl or towels under the leak and stop using the sink.

Never put your hand into the disposal, even when it is off, and never use tongs or a spoon with the power on. Garbage disposal repair means freeing a jam, resetting the motor, fixing the seals and hoses or replacing the unit, and the symptom tells a plumber which one it is.

What each sound or leak means

Listen and look before anything gets taken apart.

Humming, no spin

The blades are jammed. Common culprits are bone, fruit pits, a bottle cap or a spoon that slid in.

Silent, no hum

The reset button has tripped, a wire is loose, or the motor is dead. The breaker is checked first.

Leaks from the top

The sink flange seal or mounting ring has failed. This is the most common repair and often fixable.

Leaks from the side

A loose dishwasher hose or drain connection, tightened or replaced.

Leaks from the bottom

A cracked housing or failed internal seal. Replace the unit.

Smell that will not leave

Food film inside the grinding chamber or the drain trap behind it, not the motor.

A safe way to try a reset

Only if the unit is off at the switch and the breaker.

  1. 1

    Cut power

    Turn the wall switch and the breaker off. Do not rely on the switch alone.

  2. 2

    Turn the blades by hand

    Many disposals have a hex socket on the underside. A hex key lets you rock the blades back and forth to free a jam without touching them.

  3. 3

    Remove the obstruction

    Use tongs or pliers from above, never fingers. Take out whatever you can see.

  4. 4

    Press reset

    Wait a few minutes for the motor to cool, restore power, press the red reset button on the bottom, and run cold water as you test it.

The dishwasher connection

On most kitchens the dishwasher drain hose connects to a port on the disposal. If a new disposal was installed and the knockout plug inside that port was never removed, the dishwasher will not drain. If the port is clogged with food, water backs up from the sink into the dishwasher.

Fixing it means clearing or knocking out the plug, replacing the hose clamp, or cleaning the port. If the dishwasher still backs up with the disposal cleared, the problem is further down and a drain cleaning visit can clear the branch line.

Older Covington kitchens and the drain behind the disposal

Covington's population peaked at 65,252 in 1930, and many kitchen drain lines in older homes are narrow, galvanized or cast iron with a lot of internal scale. A disposal pushes ground food into that line, and old pipe snags grease and particles. If your disposal keeps backing up, the line behind it is likely the real issue.

Covington and Newport have a combined sewer system that carries wastewater and stormwater, according to Sanitation District No. 1. Grease poured down a disposal still ends up in that system, so wipe pans before rinsing. If sewage backs up in the lowest drain in the house, see our sewer line cleaning page.
