Toilet Installation in Covington, KY: A Step-by-Step Guide

A toilet swap is one of the few plumbing jobs a careful homeowner can finish in an afternoon. Most of the work is checking what is under the old toilet before the new one goes down.

By the Covington 24/7 Plumbing teamPublished 2026-10-13Updated 2026-10-138 min read

The short version of installing a toilet

To install a toilet, shut off the water, drain and lift the old bowl, scrape the flange clean, set a new wax ring, lower the new bowl onto the closet bolts, tighten it down evenly, then connect the supply line. Most swaps take two to three hours. The part that decides whether the job lasts is the flange, the ring of metal or plastic on the floor that the bowl bolts to.

If the flange is cracked, rusted through, or sitting below the finished floor, a new toilet will rock and leak no matter how carefully you set it. Check that first, and the rest of the job is straightforward.

Gather these before you shut the water off

Running to the hardware store with the bathroom out of service is the usual way a one-afternoon job becomes a two-day one.

  • ✓Measure the rough-in, the distance from the wall to the center of the floor drain. Twelve inches is common, but ten and fourteen exist.
  • ✓A new wax ring, or a wax-free seal if you prefer, plus a new set of closet bolts.
  • ✓A braided stainless supply line, 3/8 inch, long enough to reach without a tight bend.
  • ✓A putty knife, a bucket, a sponge, old towels, and a few rags to plug the drain opening.
  • ✓Plastic shims in case the floor is slightly out of level.
  • ✓A helper. A full toilet weighs 60 to 100 pounds, and the bowl alone is awkward to lift.

Pulling the old toilet and setting the new one

Work slowly and never crank on porcelain. It cracks without warning.

  1. 1

    Shut off and drain

    Close the stop valve behind the toilet by turning it clockwise. Flush, hold the handle down, and sponge out what remains in the tank and bowl. Disconnect the supply line and keep a towel under the fitting.

  2. 2

    Lift and inspect the flange

    Pop the bolt caps, remove the nuts, and rock the bowl gently to break the old seal. Lift straight up and set it on newspaper. Stuff a rag into the drain opening to block sewer gas, then scrape every trace of old wax off the flange.

  3. 3

    Set the ring and the bowl

    Install new closet bolts in the flange slots and press the new wax ring onto the bottom of the bowl or the flange, depending on the product. Lower the bowl straight down over the bolts in one motion. Do not lift it again once it touches the ring.

  4. 4

    Tighten, connect, test

    Press down with your body weight, then thread the nuts on by hand. Tighten each side a quarter turn at a time, alternating, until the bowl is firm. Connect the supply line, open the valve slowly, and flush five times while checking the base and the connections for moisture.

What the flange tells you

The flange is the hidden part of every toilet job. Here is what a flashlight and a few seconds of looking can reveal.

Flange sits below the floor

After a tile or vinyl upgrade, the ring can end up lower than the surface. The wax ring cannot bridge the gap, so the seal leaks. A thicker ring or a flange extender solves it.

Cracked or broken ring

Plastic flanges crack and cast iron ones rust. A repair ring screwed to the subfloor works only if the floor underneath is solid.

Bolt slots are chewed up

Stripped slots let the bowl shift. A rocking toilet breaks its wax seal over time, which is how a slow leak starts.

Soft or stained subfloor

Press the area with a screwdriver tip. If it sinks, the wood is rotted from a long, quiet leak. Stop the install and have the floor repaired first.

Wrong rough-in

A bowl built for a 12-inch rough-in will not sit properly on a 10-inch drain. Measure again before you buy.

Mistakes that cause the call-back

Over-tightening is the classic. Porcelain does not flex, and a bowl cranked down on one side can crack at the base months later. Snug and even beats tight.

Reusing the old wax ring is the second. Wax does not spring back after it has been compressed, and a bowl that has been lifted and reset on the same ring often weeps at the base. A new ring costs very little compared with a stained ceiling underneath.

Skipping the level check comes third. A bowl that leans forward or sideways stresses the seal and the tank bolts. Shim the low corner, then trim the shims flush after the caulk line is set.

Safety notes for a bathroom job

Wear gloves. Old wax, residue, and the water in the trap carry bacteria, and a spent bowl is not clean even when it looks it. Wash up afterward and keep children and pets out of the room until the floor is dry.

Never use a power tool near standing water without a GFCI-protected outlet. If you smell gas anywhere in the home, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 before you do anything else, and call a plumber afterward.

When the install is bigger than a swap

If the new toilet goes in a different spot, the drain and vent have to move with it. That is new work, not a replacement, and the Kentucky Division of Plumbing lists moving fixtures among the jobs that need a permit. Permit rules vary, so ask the Division of Plumbing or the local plumbing inspector before you cut anything.

A licensed plumber can pull the permit, run the drain at the right slope, and arrange for any covered work to be inspected before it is closed up. For planned jobs like this one, new fixture and bathroom installation is the better route than a patched-together weekend.

A toilet that still misbehaves afterward

A new bowl should flush cleanly with one push. If it runs, whistles, or leaves water around the base, the fault is usually a fill valve, a flapper, or a seal that did not seat. Our guide to fixing running and leaking toilets covers those.

Gurgling from other drains when you flush is a different signal. It suggests the vent or the drain line is partly blocked, and a new toilet will not cure it.
