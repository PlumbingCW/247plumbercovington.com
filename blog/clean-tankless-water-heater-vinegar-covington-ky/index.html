How to Clean a Tankless Water Heater With Vinegar in Covington, KY

Scale on the heat exchanger is the usual reason a tankless unit runs weak or throws errors. A vinegar flush removes it in about an hour, if your unit allows it.

By the Covington 24/7 Plumbing teamPublished 2026-10-13Updated 2026-10-138 min read

The short answer: a vinegar flush, if the manual allows it

To clean a tankless water heater with vinegar, you shut off power and gas, close the water valves, hook a small pump and two hoses to the service ports, and circulate white vinegar through the heat exchanger for about 45 to 60 minutes. Then you rinse with clean water until the smell is gone. Most units made for this have two isolation valves with flush ports built in.

Check your owner manual first. A few makers specify their own descaling solution or a different time, and some forbid vinegar on certain seals. If the manual is gone, the model number on the unit's data plate will find it online in a minute.

Signs the heat exchanger is scaling up

Scale builds when heated water drops dissolved minerals onto the inside of the heat exchanger. The coating acts like insulation, so the burner or element works harder for less heat. Over months the unit may take longer to warm up, swing between hot and lukewarm, or shut down with an error code.

You may also hear popping or rumbling as water boils in tiny pockets under the scale. Low flow at the taps, even when the cold side runs fine, is another clue. If you see none of these, you can skip the flush and just check the inlet screen.

What to gather before you start

Having everything on the floor beside the unit keeps the job short and dry.

  • ✓A small submersible pump rated for hot liquid and two washing-machine style hoses with the right fittings.
  • ✓About 3 gallons of plain white vinegar (5 percent acidity), plus a 5-gallon bucket.
  • ✓A towel, a flashlight, and a second bucket for the drain water.
  • ✓Your owner manual, open to the maintenance page.
  • ✓Safety glasses and gloves, because vinegar stings eyes and small cuts.
  • ✓A garden hose or clean-water source for the final rinse.

The flush, one stage at a time

Work in this order. Skipping the shutoff stage is the one mistake that can hurt you.

  1. 1

    Kill power and gas

    Unplug an electric unit or switch off its breaker. On a gas unit, close the gas valve and unplug the power cord. Let the unit cool so the first drain water is not scalding.

  2. 2

    Isolate the unit

    Close the hot and cold isolation valves, then open the pressure relief ports on both to release pressure. Place the bucket under the ports, since a little water always escapes.

  3. 3

    Circulate the vinegar

    Attach one hose from the pump to the cold-side port and the other from the hot-side port back into the bucket of vinegar. Open the ports and run the pump for 45 to 60 minutes. Use the longer time if the vinegar comes back cloudy.

  4. 4

    Rinse and restart

    Drain the vinegar, remove the hoses, and close the ports. Open the cold valve and run water through the unit for 5 minutes. Clean the inlet screen, restore gas and power, and open a hot tap to purge air.

One number worth knowing

The US Department of Energy puts a tankless water heater's life at about 20 years, versus 10 to 15 for a storage tank. Regular flushing is a large part of reaching that number. A unit that never gets descaled can fail years early.

Mistakes that cost people a heat exchanger

Most damage comes from rushing or improvising.

Using bleach or a harsh cleaner

Never mix vinegar with bleach, since the fumes are dangerous. Stick to plain white vinegar unless the maker names another product.

Forgetting to rinse

Leftover vinegar can leave a sour taste and pit some seals. Flush with clean water until the smell is gone.

Running the pump dry

A pump with no liquid overheats in minutes. Keep both hose ends under the vinegar surface.

Reopening the gas first

Restore water and fill the unit before gas and power. Firing an empty heat exchanger can crack it.

Chasing every error code

A flush clears scale, not a failed fan or sensor. If the code returns, stop and have it diagnosed.

How often to flush, and what shortens the gap

Many manufacturers suggest a flush about once a year. The right interval depends on how mineral-heavy your water is, and no verified hardness number exists for this site, so please do not trust a guess. Test your tap with a cheap hardness strip and ask the water utility for its annual water quality report.

If the strip shows hard water, flush more often and consider a scale-control filter on the cold inlet. Households that run several showers at once also load the unit harder. A plumber doing a yearly check can pair the flush with a look at the venting, the filter screen, and the condensate drain on high-efficiency models.

Gas units and safety, in plain words

A gas tankless heater has a burner, a vent, and a gas line. If you smell gas at any point, do not touch a switch, do not light anything. Leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber.

Never flush with the vent cap off or the cover removed beyond what the manual shows. Do not trust a unit that shows soot, scorch marks, or a yellow flame. Those point to a venting or combustion problem that vinegar will not touch, and a licensed plumber should look at it the same day.

When the vinegar does not help

If the flush brings back full flow but the water still runs lukewarm, the fault is elsewhere. A cross-connected faucet, a failed flow sensor, or a mixing valve in the line can all copy the symptoms of scale. A plumber checks inlet temperature, flow rate, and the error history before touching parts.

Cracked heat exchangers and failing fans usually mean repair or replacement. Our water heater repair work covers diagnosis for both tank and tankless units, and a plumber can tell you honestly whether the unit is worth fixing at its age.
