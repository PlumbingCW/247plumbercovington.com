Battery Backup Sump Pump Installation in Covington, KY

A battery backup sump pump keeps water leaving your basement when the power goes out. Here is how the system works, how it is installed, and what keeps it ready.

Works in outagesSeparate backup pumpBattery and alarmLicensed plumbers
By the Covington 24/7 Plumbing teamPublished 2026-10-13Updated 2026-10-138 min read

Why the pump quits when you need it most

Heavy rain and power outages arrive together. A standard sump pump runs on household current, so a downed line or tripped breaker stops it just as the pit fills. A battery backup sump pump is a second pump, wired to a deep-cycle battery and its own float switch, that takes over when the main pump cannot run.

Power is not the only reason pumps stop. Stuck floats, clogged intakes, and worn motors fail on sunny days too. A backup also covers those cases, because it sits slightly higher in the pit and starts when water climbs past the main pump's reach.

How a plumber installs a backup

The work is usually a few hours when the pit is in good shape.

  1. 1

    Inspect the pit and discharge

    The plumber checks pit size, the check valve, and the discharge line. A pit under about 18 inches across may not hold two pumps.

  2. 2

    Set the backup higher

    The backup pump sits on a base or bracket so its float triggers a few inches above the main pump's float. That way it only runs when the primary fails or is overwhelmed.

  3. 3

    Run a separate discharge

    Best practice is a second discharge pipe, with its own check valve, so the two pumps do not push against each other. Where one line is unavoidable, a proper tee fitting is used.

  4. 4

    Wire, charge, and test

    The controller connects to the battery and plugs into an outlet. The plumber tests the pump by filling the pit and then unplugging the main pump to prove the backup starts.

What to ask for in the quote

Details that separate a lasting setup from a weak one.

A deep-cycle battery

A car battery is built for short bursts. A marine or AGM deep-cycle battery tolerates the long drain a pump needs.

A high-water alarm

An audible alarm, or a phone alert on a smart controller, tells you the pit is rising before the floor floods.

A vented battery spot

Flooded lead-acid batteries release gas while charging. Keep them in a ventilated place, up off the floor.

A pump sized for the job

Capacity is measured in gallons per hour at a given lift. Ask the plumber how they sized it for your discharge height.

A tested check valve

A check valve stops pumped water from falling back into the pit. A worn one makes the pump cycle all day.

A runtime reality check

A fully charged deep-cycle battery can run a backup pump for several hours of continuous pumping, but far longer if water enters slowly and the pump cycles. A long outage in a downpour can still drain it. Ask the plumber for the expected runtime with your battery and pump, and plan for a way to recharge or swap.

Keep it ready: a simple maintenance routine

Ten minutes every few months beats an emergency call.

  • ✓Pour a bucket of water into the pit every three months and watch both pumps start.
  • ✓Unplug the main pump on purpose once a year and confirm the backup runs.
  • ✓Check the battery terminals for white crust and clean them with a baking soda paste.
  • ✓Look at the controller lights and read what they say about charge state.
  • ✓Replace the battery on the schedule in the manual, often every three to five years.
  • ✓Clear leaves and grit from the pit and the discharge outlet outside.

Mistakes that make backups fail

The biggest is never testing. Owners install a system, forget about it, and find a dead battery during the first storm. Another is wiring both pumps to the same float, so one stuck switch disables both.

Freezing is a second trap. A discharge line that ends in a buried or capped outlet can ice shut in a cold snap. Keep the outlet clear, and ask your plumber whether a frost-protected outlet fits your yard.

What a sump pump, even a backed-up one, will not do

A sump pump moves groundwater out of a pit. It has no link to the city sewer. If your floor drain or toilet backs up in a storm, the cause is in the sewer, and a bigger pump will not help.

Water coming through the wall in large amounts may also need a better drain around the footing. Our basement waterproofing service pairs a drain system with the pump. If the pit itself is old or noisy, a visit from our sump pump repair team can tell you whether to repair, replace, or add the backup.

Electrical safety around a flooding pit

Never reach into a pit while a pump is plugged in, and never stand in water to reset a breaker. If water is near outlets or the panel, stay out, shut off power only from a dry spot, and call. Keep the area clear of children and pets.

Battery systems carry power even when the house is dark. Treat the terminals as live, and keep tools and jewelry away from them.
