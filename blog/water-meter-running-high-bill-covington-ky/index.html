Water Meter Running With Everything Off? Finding the Leak Behind a High Bill

If the meter moves while no one is using water, something is leaking. A two-hour test confirms it, and a short walk-through usually narrows down where.

By the Covington 24/7 Plumbing teamPublished 2026-10-13Updated 2026-10-138 min read

The quick answer: a moving meter means water is leaving

When every tap, appliance, and hose is off and the meter still moves, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and the fixtures. Most of the time it is a toilet, followed by a dripping faucet, a leaking water line, or an irrigation fault. The cause is findable, and the sooner you start, the less you pay for water that goes nowhere.

The US Environmental Protection Agency says the average household leaks more than 9,300 gallons of water per year, and about nine percent of homes leak 50 gallons or more per day. A bill that jumped without a change in habits is a good reason to test.

Run the meter test

This is the EPA method. It takes two hours and no tools.

  1. 1

    Shut everything off

    Close every tap, and make sure the dishwasher, washer, ice maker, and humidifier are not running. Stop any irrigation timer.

  2. 2

    Read the meter

    Lift the meter box lid. Write down the full reading, and note whether the small low-flow dial or triangle is spinning.

  3. 3

    Wait two hours

    Use no water at all. Do not flush a toilet, and tell everyone in the house.

  4. 4

    Read it again

    Any change in the reading means a leak is likely. If the dial still spins at the start, you do not even need to wait.

Where the water usually hides

Check these in order, because the top ones are the cheapest to fix.

A silent toilet leak

A worn flapper lets water seep into the bowl with no sound. Add food coloring to the tank. If color reaches the bowl in 10 minutes without flushing, the flapper is leaking.

Dripping faucets and showerheads

A faucet dripping once per second wastes more than 3,000 gallons a year, according to the EPA.

A water heater relief valve

A small dribble at the discharge pipe can run all day. Check the pipe end for moisture.

The line from meter to house

A leak underground shows as a soggy patch, a green strip of lawn, or low pressure.

Irrigation and outdoor taps

A valve stuck open or a cracked sprinkler head can run water for days.

Isolate the leak with the main valve

Once you see movement, close the main shutoff valve where the line enters the house and watch the meter. If it stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is on the buried line between the meter and your foundation.

That second finding changes the job. Our water line repair crew handles the buried run, and a detection visit finds the spot first so the dig stays small.

One caution about the meter itself

The meter and its box belong to the water utility. Read and look, but do not turn, tap, or open the valve on the utility side. If the box lid is stuck, flooded, or the dial is cracked, call the utility.

When the meter is moving and nothing visible is wrong

Hidden leaks sit in walls, under slabs, and behind tile. Clues include a warm spot on the floor, a musty smell, peeling paint, or water pressure that fell for no reason. Hot water lines in a slab often show as a warm floor patch.

A plumber confirms the spot with acoustic listening, thermal imaging, or a pressure test, and then opens as little as possible. A planned leak detection visit costs less than guessing with a hammer.

Before you call: five minutes that save time

These notes let a plumber work faster.

  • ✓Write down the meter reading and the time, before and after the test.
  • ✓Note whether the low-flow dial moved with the main shut.
  • ✓Photograph the wet spots, bills, and the meter dial.
  • ✓List any changes: new appliance, irrigation start-up, recent freeze.
  • ✓Move furniture and boxes away from suspect walls and the water heater.
  • ✓Keep the main shutoff unblocked so anyone can reach it.

Mistakes that keep the bill high

The first is waiting for next month's bill to confirm the problem. Another is assuming a high bill must be the utility's error, without testing. A third is shutting the main off and forgetting to open it again.

People also ignore the water heater. A relief valve leak or a slow drip into a floor drain is easy to miss. Check it before you pay for deeper searching.

Talking to the water utility about the bill

Call the water utility, give them the meter reading and the date of your test, and ask whether the meter has been checked recently. Bring bill dates so the pattern is clear. Ask what their policy is for leak-related bills, since rules differ and a plumber cannot speak for the utility.

Keep the plumber's invoice and notes on the repair. If you need to explain the high usage, those papers show you found and fixed the cause.
