Why Is My Tankless Water Heater Leaking? A Covington, KY Guide

Most tankless leaks come from a loose fitting, a worn valve seal, or condensation, and a few come from a cracked heat exchanger. Where the water appears tells you which.

If it is leaking right now, do this first

Stop the water, then work out why. Use these four moves in order.

Close the cold supply

Turn the cold inlet valve under the unit a quarter turn to stop water entering. If you cannot reach it, close the main water valve for the house.

Cut the power

Unplug the unit or switch off its breaker, but only from dry footing. Do not touch the unit or any outlet while standing in water.

Close the gas valve

On a gas model, close the gas valve to the unit. If you smell gas at any point, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 before anything else.

Catch and photograph

Put a pan or towels down, photograph the leak and the wet area, and note when it started. A plumber and an insurer will both ask.

The short answer: read the drip location

A tankless water heater leaks for four main reasons. Fittings loosen, internal seals wear out, condensation drains the wrong way, or the heat exchanger cracks. Each shows up in a different spot, so look before you guess.

Dry the floor, put a paper towel under each fitting, and check again in an hour. A drip at the bottom connections is the easy case. A leak from the case itself, or water running down from inside, points at a harder fix.

Where the water shows up, and what it means

Match your leak to the closest case.

Bottom connections

The water and gas fittings at the base can loosen from heat cycling. A worn washer or union is a short repair.

Isolation valve ports

Service valves have rubber O-rings that dry out over years. If you recently flushed the unit, an O-ring may not be seated.

Pressure relief valve outlet

A drip here can mean high water pressure or a failing valve. Either needs attention, and the valve should never be capped.

Condensate drain or trap

High-efficiency units make acidic condensate that drains through a small tube. A clogged tube sends it onto the floor.

Inside the case

Water running from the front panel or vent means an internal leak. A cracked heat exchanger is the worst answer and usually ends the unit's life.

Condensation is not always a leak

Condensing tankless models pull so much heat from exhaust that water vapor turns to liquid inside the unit. That water is mildly acidic and runs to a drain through a small plastic line, sometimes through a neutralizer cartridge. If the line kinks or fills with debris, it spills.

Short bursts of dripping from the exhaust or a damp spot near the vent can be normal in cold weather. A steady stream is not normal. If you cannot tell, a plumber can check the slope of the vent and the condensate path.

How old is the unit?

The US Department of Energy puts a tankless water heater's life at about 20 years. A leak in a unit near that age often means the heat exchanger is giving out, and replacing it can make more sense than another repair. In a younger unit, the cause is usually a fitting or seal.

What you can check without tools

Do these only after the unit is shut off and dry.

  • ✓Look at the hot and cold pipes for wet spots, green crust, or rust at joints.
  • ✓Check the relief valve outlet for a drip trail on the pipe or wall below.
  • ✓Feel the floor under the unit for warmth, which may mean a hidden line.
  • ✓Look at the condensate tube for kinks, clogs, or a missing drain connection.
  • ✓Read the error code on the display and write it down.
  • ✓Check the water pressure with a gauge on an outdoor spigot, if you have one.

Mistakes that turn a drip into a bigger job

Over-tightening a fitting cracks it. Wrapping tape around a leaking joint hides the problem for a week. Capping a relief valve outlet is dangerous, since the valve exists to protect the unit from over-pressure.

The other mistake is waiting. A slow drip near the electrical board can short it, and a leaking gas unit may not vent correctly if water reaches the burner. A 10-minute call now is cheaper than a ruined control board.

Repair or replace: how a plumber decides

The plumber looks at age, leak source, parts availability, and the unit's service history. A fitting leak on a unit under ten years old is nearly always worth fixing. A cracked heat exchanger on a unit past fifteen rarely is.

If a replacement is the right call, ask about venting, gas line size, and whether the old isolation valves can stay. Our tankless water heater service covers new installs and our water heater repair crew handles diagnosis first, so you only replace when the facts say so.

Why minerals matter for leaks

Scale does more than waste energy. It can stress seals and valves, and sections of a heat exchanger that overheat under scale can crack. A yearly flush is cheap insurance.

There is no verified hardness figure to quote for local water. Test your own tap with a hardness strip and ask the water utility for its annual water quality report, then plan the flush interval from that.
