About Covington 24/7 Plumbing

We are a phone-first plumbing service at 33 E 7th St in Covington, KY. This page explains how a call works, what we do and what we leave to others.

A plumbing service you reach by phone

Covington 24/7 Plumbing is built around one thing: a person answering the phone when a pipe, drain or water heater fails. There are no booking pages to wade through and nothing to type into a screen at midnight. You call, you describe the problem, and licensed plumbers take it from there.

The office address is 33 E 7th St, Covington, KY 41011. The work happens at your property, in Covington and in the neighboring Kenton County towns listed further down. This site does not name individual plumbers, quote customer numbers or list awards. What it offers is a plain description of how the work is done, so you can judge it yourself.

How a call works from first ring to finished repair

The same four stages apply to a burst pipe and to a planned fixture swap, only the speed differs.

  1. 1

    Dispatch answers

    The 24/7 emergency line is answered at any hour. You describe what you see, hear and smell, and the dispatcher asks about water, gas and electrical hazards first.

  2. 2

    Immediate steps by phone

    Before anyone drives out, you may be asked to close the main valve, shut off the water heater or stop using drains. Those steps limit damage while the visit is arranged.

  3. 3

    A licensed plumber diagnoses

    The plumber looks for the cause, not only the symptom. A wet ceiling might come from a supply line, a drain, a roof or a condensation line, and each one needs a different repair.

  4. 4

    A clear price, then the work

    You hear the price before work starts. If the plumber finds something different from what was described on the phone, the price is discussed again before anything changes.

What we do not do

We do not promise an arrival time before a dispatcher knows your address, your problem and where crews are. A promise made too early is a promise that gets broken. You will get an honest window instead.

We do not give a price over the phone for a problem nobody has seen, because the same symptom can have several causes. We do not ask for card numbers on this website, and we do not work from an online request. Septic tanks and sewer problems on properties without a public sewer connection are best handled by a septic specialist, and we will say so plainly.

How to verify a Kentucky plumber

Kentucky regulates plumbing through the Division of Plumbing, part of the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction. Homeowners can verify a plumber's license on the division's website at dhbc.ky.gov/plb. It is worth doing for any contractor, including us, before a large job begins.

The division also runs the Kentucky State Plumbing Code, which KRS 318.130 requires, and employs the state plumbing inspectors. Its homeowner packet says that new installations, moved fixtures, water service from the meter to the house and house sewers need a plumbing permit.

It also says work covered by concrete, inside walls or underground must be inspected before it is covered, and pressure tests are held for 15 minutes. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Division of Plumbing or the local plumbing inspector.

That is why a good plumber talks about permits and inspections early, before a trench is dug or a wall is closed. If a contractor waves the question away, treat it as a reason to ask more.

Where the work happens

Covington sits at the meeting point of the Ohio and Licking Rivers, with 20,576 housing units counted in 2020 and nineteen neighborhoods, many within twelve historic districts. Older buildings mean older pipe, and that shapes which problems show up first.

Past the city line the same line serves Fort Wright, Fort Mitchell, Ludlow, Park Hills and Taylor Mill. The service area page explains how ZIP codes work across those towns.

Insurance and responsibility

The plumbers are licensed and insured. For your own protection, take photos of any water damage and keep paperwork from the visit, because homeowner and landlord insurance treat sudden bursts differently from slow leaks.

Who pays for what in a rental depends on the lease and on state law. If you rent, tell the owner or property manager promptly. In a true emergency, stop the water first and sort out responsibility afterward.
