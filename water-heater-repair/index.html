When a leaking water heater is an emergency

A water heater that is leaking in Covington, KY is an emergency once water is pooling, running from the bottom of the tank, or spreading toward outlets and appliances. Our 24/7 emergency line is open for exactly this. While you wait, close the cold water valve on the pipe above the tank and switch the heater off, at the breaker for electric units or at the gas control for gas units.

If you smell gas, leave the building first. Call your gas utility or 911 from outside, and only then call a plumber.

What the symptom usually points to

Most calls start with one of these six complaints, and each has a short list of likely causes.

No hot water at all

On a gas unit, look at a pilot that went out, a worn thermocouple or a dead gas valve. On an electric unit, suspect a tripped breaker, a high-limit reset switch or both elements burned out.

Warm, then cold fast

A cracked dip tube sends cold supply water straight to the outlet. One failed upper or lower element on an electric tank gives the same short supply.

Water too hot

A stuck thermostat or a bad gas control keeps heating past the setpoint. The DOE suggests 120 F as a safe and efficient setting.

Rumbling or popping

Sediment on the tank bottom traps steam bubbles under it. Flushing helps early on, but heavy buildup can crack the lining over time.

Rusty hot water only

If cold taps run clear and hot taps run brown, the tank interior is corroding. The anode rod may be spent, or the tank may be near the end.

Dripping pipe on the side

That pipe is the discharge line from the temperature and pressure relief valve. A steady drip means the valve is worn or the system pressure is running too high.

Is the water from the tank or from a fitting?

Dry the heater with a towel, then press a fresh paper towel to each suspect spot. Wet at the pipe connections on top, the drain valve, or the relief valve points to a part that can be replaced. Wet that returns from under the jacket, or a steady puddle from the base, usually means the tank wall itself has failed.

The EPA says household leaks waste more than 9,300 gallons a year on average, and a slow tank leak often goes unseen behind a utility closet door. Check the floor beside the heater once a month. If water seems to come from somewhere else in the house, a leak detection visit can trace it.

Gas, electric and hybrid units fail in different places

The type of heater decides which parts get tested first.

Gas storage tanks

Burner, pilot assembly, thermocouple, gas control valve and venting are the common suspects. A blocked flue is a safety problem, not just a comfort one, so a unit that keeps shutting itself off gets a vent check.

Electric storage tanks

Two heating elements and two thermostats share the work, so one dead element often leaves you with a little warm water. A plumber tests resistance at each element and checks the reset button before pulling anything.

Hybrid heat pump units

These add a compressor, fan, condensate drain and electronic controls on top of the tank. A clogged condensate line or error code is often a quick fix, while sealed-system faults can cost more than they are worth on an older unit.

No hot water: a quick check before you call

Look for the simple causes first. On an electric heater, check the double-pole breaker and press the red reset button on the upper thermostat once. On a gas heater, see whether the pilot is lit and whether the gas valve on the supply line is open.

If a reset trips again right away, stop. That pattern points to a shorted element or a wiring fault, and repeated resetting only masks it. Our 24/7 line takes those calls at night and on weekends, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

Older Covington housing and what it means for the tank

Covington counted 20,576 housing units in 2020, and its population peaked at 65,252 in 1930. That means many homes in the historic districts, including Mainstrasse Village, have had several water heaters over the decades, often in basements or tight closets. Ask the plumber to look at the venting and the supply shutoff valve while the heater is open, because those older rooms may carry decades-old connections.

Northern Kentucky Water District serves 99 percent of Kenton County households, so most heaters here run on NKWD water. The sediment your tank collects depends on your own supply, so test your tap and ask NKWD for its annual water quality report instead of guessing. When the repair bill says replace

A tank with a failed wall cannot be patched, and a tank in its last years may not justify a new gas valve. In those cases, a full replacement takes less total money than repairing and then replacing in a few months.
