What a 1910 city means for your pipes

Fort Mitchell was incorporated more than a century ago, though individual houses were built across many decades after that. A home from an earlier era may still carry galvanized steel supply lines, which corrode from the inside and cut pressure. If the water from one tap sputters or runs brown after sitting, mention it on the call.

Newer additions tell a different story. A bathroom added in a later decade may share a drain with older plumbing, and the join between old and new is a common leak point. A plumber can check it with a quick pressure test.

Problems we see across Fort Mitchell houses

General guidance, not a verdict on your property.

Failing water heaters

The US Department of Energy says storage tanks last 10 to 15 years. A puddle at the base means the tank itself may have rusted through.

Corroded supply pipe

Low pressure at every faucet, or orange tint after a day away, points to older metal pipe.

Hidden leaks

A meter that moves with every fixture off is the clearest sign. The EPA says household leaks waste over 9,300 gallons per year on average.

Dripping faucets

A faucet that drips once per second wastes more than 3,000 gallons a year, says the EPA.

Slow kitchen drains

Grease builds up in older kitchen lines and slows the sink long before it stops.

If you have a burst pipe or active leak in Fort Mitchell

Take these steps while the 24/7 line gets a plumber moving.

Shut the main

Turn the main valve clockwise to stop the flow.

Kill the heater

Switch off power or gas control to the water heater so the tank does not heat empty.

Move belongings

Lift rugs, boxes and electronics above the wet floor.

Stay away from outlets

Do not touch cords or switches near standing water.
