Sewer Camera Inspection in Covington, KY

A sewer camera inspection feeds a waterproof camera through your drain line to show roots, cracks, sags and breaks on video. Licensed plumbers scope sewer lines in Covington, KY.

Why a plumber would put a camera in your sewer line

Sewer line inspection answers one question: what is the condition of the pipe between your house and the street? The line is buried, often under a yard, a driveway or a sidewalk, so nobody sees it until something goes wrong. A camera on a flexible rod, about the diameter of a coin, travels down the pipe and sends live video to a monitor.

For homeowners in Covington, KY the inspection is useful before buying, after a repeat clog, or before spending money on digging. It replaces a guess with a picture.

The drain keeps stopping up, the yard dipped, the basement smells

These are the symptoms that usually lead to a scope.

Same clog, third cable

If a cable clears it and the line slows again within weeks, something in the pipe is catching debris. The camera finds it.

Soft or sinking ground

A low spot above the line, or a sinkhole in the yard or street, can mean the pipe has failed and soil is washing in.

Smell of sewage outside

A strong odor near the house or cleanout may be a crack, a broken joint or a trap that has dried out.

Slow drains in a new-to-you home

Previous owners rarely mention a line that backs up in wet weather. The scope tells you before you learn the hard way.

How a sewer camera inspection works

The plumber enters the line through a cleanout or by pulling a toilet and pushes the camera down the pipe slowly. A distance counter shows how far the head has traveled, so a defect at 38 feet is marked at 38 feet. A transmitter in the camera head can be picked up with a handheld locator above ground, which lets the plumber mark the spot on the lawn.

That marking is called a locate. It matters when a repair is coming, because digging at the correct spot can save a lot of unneeded excavation. Ask for the footage and depth of any problem along with the video.

Who owns the pipe, and what SD1 says

Sanitation District No. 1 (SD1) describes the private lateral as the pipe from the building plumbing to the connection with its sewer, and the property owner owns and maintains it. That means a crack found by the camera on your side of the connection is your repair. SD1 has also run a program for defective laterals under public roadways, tied to pavement failure, building backup or overflow, and you should confirm whether it is available by calling SD1 at 859-578-7450.

Covington also has a combined sewer, one pipe carrying wastewater and storm water, in its older areas. If the camera shows a clean, sound lateral but your basement still backs up during storms, the issue is not your pipe. Having the video in hand makes that call to SD1 much more useful.

Scoping before you buy an older house

Home inspectors look at what is visible, and a standard inspection does not include the sewer line. Covington reached its peak population of 65,252 in 1930, and many of its houses are old enough to carry original or early replacement lines. General guidance: homes built before about 1960 can still have clay or cast iron lateral pipes that deserve a look.

Ask for a video of the whole run from the house to the connection. If the camera shows roots or an offset, you can negotiate repairs, ask the seller to address them or plan the work. A scope before closing costs far less than a surprise afterward, and it can be done during the inspection window.

The same scope is available for buyers in Park Hills and the other towns around Covington, where houses of different ages carry different pipe.

Reading the video with your plumber

Four things to look for as the footage plays.

  1. 1

    Pipe material

    Clay looks tan and jointed every few feet. Cast iron looks dark and rough. Plastic looks smooth and light.

  2. 2

    Joints

    Roots enter at joints as white hairs. A joint that is offset or open is a repair candidate.

  3. 3

    Water line

    A pool of standing water in the pipe marks a belly. A short one may be tolerable and a long one often is not.

  4. 4

    Cracks and breaks

    A crack along the pipe or a missing section shows soil or daylight. This is when repair or relining comes into the discussion.
