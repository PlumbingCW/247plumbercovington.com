Plumber in Old Seminary Square, Covington, KY

Old Seminary Square is one of Covington's smallest neighborhoods, and one of its most carefully restored. Plumbing work here often happens inside homes that have been renovated more than once.

Victorian rehab areaEighth to 12th StreetTwo neighborhood parks

A small neighborhood with a revival story

The Covington Neighborhood Collaborative describes Old Seminary Square as bounded by the CSX tracks on the east, Banklick Street on the west, 12th Street on the south and Eighth Street on the north, with Russell Street running through the middle. It calls it one of the smallest neighborhoods in Covington by map area.

The same page says Italianate Victorian homes are the main housing type. Absentee landlords had divided many into apartments, and in the mid-1970s about a dozen people began buying and restoring them. Two parks serve the area: Basil Lewis Park at 118 W 10th Street and Annie Hargraves Park at 122 W Robbins Street.

Plumbing questions that come with restored and divided houses

General guidance. Every house is different.

Plumbing from several eras

A house that was divided, then restored, can have supply and drain lines installed at different times. A camera or an access panel shows what is actually there.

Upgrades behind finished walls

After a restoration, plaster and trim are worth protecting. Ask for a leak to be pinpointed so the opening is small.

Shared stacks in converted units

If a house still holds more than one unit, find out which fixtures drain into the same stack before a clog turns into a problem for two households.

Water heater placement

Tanks tucked into closets or basement corners can leak a long time unseen. Look at the floor beneath yours every few months.

Sewer and water basics for this part of Covington

Sanitation District No. 1 says older areas of Covington have a combined sewer, which carries wastewater and storm water in one pipe. The owner is responsible for the lateral from the house to the district's sewer. If a drain backs up repeatedly, our sewer inspection page explains how a camera check works.

Drinking water comes from the Northern Kentucky Water District, which serves 99 percent of Kenton County households. We do not publish a hardness figure for your tap. Test your own water or ask the district for its annual report.

When water appears where it should not

A simple order of operations for a leak in a multi-floor home.

  1. 1

    Find the source floor

    Water usually shows below the leak, not at it. Check the room above the stain first.

  2. 2

    Shut the local valve

    Close the fixture shutoff if there is one, then the main if water keeps coming.

  3. 3

    Protect the woodwork

    Lift rugs, move furniture and open a window or run a fan to start drying.

  4. 4

    Photograph, then call

    Take dated photos for your insurer and describe the location, the fixture and the timing.
