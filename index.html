When the problem cannot wait until morning

Some plumbing trouble is a nuisance and some is a flood. A pipe that lets go inside a Mainstrasse Village building can put water through a ceiling in minutes, and a sewer line that backs up at midnight will not improve by sunrise. Those are the jobs an emergency plumber exists for, and the 24/7 line is staffed for them.

Call first, then follow what the dispatcher tells you for your situation. The safe first move is usually the same: find the main shutoff and close it, stop running water and flushing, and keep people and pets away from wet outlets. Everything after that is a plan instead of a panic.

Not every call is a midnight call. Same-day appointments are offered when available for work that can safely wait a few hours, like a slow drain or a dripping valve, and you hear a clear price before any work starts.

What happens after you dial

The routine is the same whether it is 2 p.m. or 2 a.m.

  1. 1

    A dispatcher picks up

    You describe what you see and hear. Gurgling, a wet ceiling or a cold shower each point the dispatcher toward a different first question.

  2. 2

    Safety comes first

    If you smell gas, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 before anything else. For water, the dispatcher walks you through the shutoff.

  3. 3

    A licensed plumber is sent

    Dispatch lines up a licensed plumber for your address. That plumber finds the cause before recommending a repair, because a guess costs more than a diagnosis.

  4. 4

    You hear the price, then decide

    The clear price comes before work starts. Bigger jobs, like a new water heater or a sewer repair, may need a permit, and permit rules vary, so the plumber tells you what applies.

Six problems worth a phone call

Each one has its own page with causes, decisions and a plain explanation of the repair.

A pipe that is leaking or split

Water in a ceiling, wall or basement means a supply line has failed. Close the main, then ask about repairing the burst pipe.

Drains backing up together

When the tub gurgles as the washer drains, the main line is the suspect. Sewer line cleaning clears the blockage and a camera shows why it formed.

A water heater that quit

A puddle under the tank or a shower that never warms up needs a diagnosis. Water heater repair fixes failed parts, and tanks past their age may call for a swap.

A meter that keeps turning

If the dial moves with every tap off, water is escaping somewhere. Leak detection locates the line without tearing up the whole yard.

A toilet that will not stop

Overflows, constant running and a rocking base all have simple causes. A visit for toilet repair usually ends the same day.

A sump pump that stopped

A basement with a dead pump can take on water fast in a storm. Sump pump repair gets the pit working before the next rain.

What Covington's pipes have in common

Covington counted 20,576 housing units in 2020, and 12.0 percent of them were vacant. A vacant building still has pressure behind its walls, so a pipe that splits in an empty unit can run for days before anyone notices. If you own or look after an empty property, a regular walk-through of the main valve and the ceilings is worth the time.

The city's population peaked at 65,252 in 1930, so many homes here date from long before modern pipe materials. Houses of that era can still carry galvanized supply lines or cast-iron drains. When a line shows its age, the plumber can price a spot fix against a full repipe and let you compare.

Covington also sits where the Licking River meets the Ohio, and its older neighborhoods share a combined sewer that carries wastewater and storm water in one pipe, according to Sanitation District No. 1. Heavy rain can overload that system and back up basements. The lateral from your building to the district's sewer belongs to you, so repeat backups deserve a look through sewer line repair options.

Before you dial the emergency plumber

Thirty seconds of preparation makes the call faster and the first visit more useful.

  • ✓Find your main water shutoff and note whether it turns freely.
  • ✓Take photos of the damage before you wipe up anything, in case you file an insurance claim.
  • ✓Write down what you hear and see, such as gurgling, hissing, a warm floor or a brown stain.
  • ✓Turn off the water heater power or gas control if the tank itself is leaking.
  • ✓Keep your street address and the nearest cross street ready.
  • ✓Clear a path to the water heater, the sink or the basement drain.

Checking a plumber before you hire one

Kentucky runs plumbing law through the Division of Plumbing, part of the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction, and homeowners can verify a plumber's license there at dhbc.ky.gov/plb. Do it before any big job. It takes a couple of minutes and settles the question.

The Kentucky State Plumbing Code, required under KRS 318.130, sets the standard that work has to meet. Work covered by concrete, hidden in walls or buried underground must be inspected before it is closed up. Read more about how a call is handled and what we do and do not do.
