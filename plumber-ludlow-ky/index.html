The 1937 flood and what it means for Ludlow basements

The Ohio River crested at 79.99 feet in Ludlow on January 26, 1937, according to the Kenton County library record. More than 43 percent of the city was underwater, over 500 families left their homes, and the city lost running water and gas. Greatest damage was to housing. The practical lesson is that a riverside home needs a plan for a wet basement and a working shutoff for every utility.

Flood stage at Cincinnati is 52 feet, and the 1937 record was 80.0 feet. A pump in the basement cannot stop a river rise, but it can handle ordinary groundwater and rain seepage when the ground is soaked.

Ludlow houses and the water problems they invite

General guidance for older homes near a river, not a finding about your address.

Seepage after heavy rain

Damp walls and a musty smell, sometimes with water along the floor edge, are common in low basements.

Sump pump failure

A pump that runs constantly or not at all will not keep up. Power loss in a storm is the usual trigger.

Aging supply pipes

Older piping can corrode and pinhole. A small spray behind a wall can run for days before anyone notices.

Floor drain backup

Water rising from a basement drain can be a sewer issue, a clog or groundwater. The source decides the fix.

Cold-snap bursts

Pipes along exterior walls freeze first and split at the weakest point.

A railroad town, a lost amusement park and old houses

Ludlow was incorporated in 1864, and the railroad arrived in the 1870s and drove its early growth. Houses built in that era have had well over a century to settle, shift and be patched. Expect a mix of materials behind the walls, and expect that a plumber will want to see the pipe before quoting.

Ludlow Lagoon opened in 1895 and closed after the 1917 season, then the lake was filled in. Only the clubhouse at 312 Lake St still stands. If you live near a filled lake bed, ground that settles unevenly can pull on buried lines, so cracks in a sewer lateral are worth a camera check.

If you have a burst pipe or active leak in Ludlow

Do these first while the 24/7 line sends help.

Cut the water

Close the main valve, then open the lowest tap to relieve pressure.

Cut the power

If water reaches outlets or the panel, stay out. Leave the breaker alone unless you can reach it dry.

Move valuables up

Raise boxes, rugs and electronics off the floor.

Check the pump

If a sump pump sits in water, do not touch it plugged in.
