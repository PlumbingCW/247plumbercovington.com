When a burst pipe is an emergency

Water spraying from a wall, pouring through a ceiling or rising across a basement floor is an emergency, and our 24/7 line takes those calls at any hour. While you wait, close the main water valve, switch off power to wet rooms from a dry breaker, and keep everyone away from standing water near outlets.

A burst pipe is a split, crack or blown joint in a supply line. Most of the damage comes before the water is shut off, so the first job is stopping the flow and the second is learning why the pipe failed.

Four moves in the first five minutes

Do these in order.

  1. 1

    Find and close the main

    The valve is usually where the line enters near the meter or the front basement wall. Turn a round handle clockwise, or turn a lever a quarter turn across the pipe.

  2. 2

    Open the lowest faucet

    Draining the system relieves pressure and lets trapped water leave the broken pipe instead of your ceiling.

  3. 3

    Kill power where it is wet

    Flip breakers for wet rooms, but only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.

  4. 4

    Start moving valuables

    Lift rugs, electronics and boxes off the floor. Then call, and tell the dispatcher what material the pipe is if you know.

Copper, PEX, galvanized and cast iron fail differently

The material tells you why it burst and how it gets repaired.

Copper

Usually splits along a seam after a freeze or gives out at a pinhole. A section is cut out and a new piece soldered or joined in.

PEX

Flexible and often survives a freeze, but fittings and kinked bends can fail. Repair is a coupling or a replaced run.

Galvanized steel

Rusts from the inside, so the wall thins and breaks at threads. If one joint failed, others often follow soon.

Cast iron

A drain material, brittle with age. It cracks across the pipe or splits at a hub and needs a cut-out and replacement section.

Frozen pipes and what happens when they thaw

A frozen pipe is not always a broken one. Ice expands about nine percent, and the split often lies a few inches from where the ice sits, so the real trouble begins when the pipe thaws and pressure pushes water through the crack.

If a faucet gives only a trickle in a hard freeze, open it, shut the main valve and warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or warm towels. Never use a torch or open flame. If you see a bulge, a frost line or any wet spot, stop and call.

Photograph the damage before you clean up

Insurers ask what the water did, so take a record first.

  • ✓Take wide and close photos of the burst pipe, the wet walls and the floor before anything moves.
  • ✓Video a slow walk through every affected room, narrating what you see.
  • ✓Keep damaged items until the insurer says they can go.
  • ✓Write down the time you noticed the water and when you closed the main.

Cracked drain pipes are a quieter version

Drain lines crack too, especially old cast iron and clay. The signs are slow instead of dramatic: a stain under a bathroom, a sewer smell, or a floor drain that backs up.

A cracked drain leaks only when water runs through it, so a camera or a fixture-by-fixture test finds it. Bigger runs may point toward sewer line repair instead of a simple patch.
