Plumber in Licking Riverside, Covington, KY

Licking Riverside mixes mansions, church halls and bungalows on the streets between Fourth and Eighth. Plumbing here often means working around good woodwork and long-standing building features.

National Register districtFourth to Eighth StreetNear the Licking River

What the National Register listing tells you

Wikipedia records the Licking Riverside Historic District as added to the National Register of Historic Places on July 30, 1975. It runs roughly from Fourth Street to Eighth Street and toward the Licking River, and it began as an expansion past the original Sixth Street edge of Covington.

The same source lists Bungalow and Craftsman, Second Empire and Italianate as the main styles. Individual landmarks include the Baker-Hunt Foundation, the Covington Art Club, the First United Methodist Church and the Covington Ladies Home, built in 1894.

Plumbing questions that come with larger and older buildings

General guidance. Age alone does not tell you what is inside the walls.

Check before you assume

Do not guess pipe material from the date on a house. A plumber can open an access point or run a camera to see what is actually there.

Big houses, long runs

A large house can have a long distance between the heater and the farthest fixture. Waiting for hot water wastes water but is not always a fault.

Converted or shared spaces

Some large buildings hold several units or offices. Know which valves serve which space before you need to shut one off.

Work behind plaster and trim

Opening finished walls is a decision, not a default. Ask for a leak to be pinpointed first so the opening stays small.

Permits and inspections on renovation work

The Kentucky Division of Plumbing says new installations, moving fixtures, water service from the meter to the house and house sewers need a plumbing permit. Work covered by concrete, inside walls or underground must be inspected before it is covered.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Division of Plumbing or the local plumbing inspector before a remodel starts. If your project touches the supply side, our repiping page explains how a whole-house job is scoped.
