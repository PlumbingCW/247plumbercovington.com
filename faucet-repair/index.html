Emergency Faucet Repair in Covington, KY

Faucet repair means fixing the cartridge, seat, seals or supply valve that controls water at a sink, tub or shower. Licensed plumbers handle it in Covington, KY, including after hours.

A faucet that will not shut off right now

If water will not stop at a faucet, or a supply line is spraying under the sink, call our 24/7 emergency line at any hour. Turn the oval handle on the supply stop under the fixture clockwise. If that handle is stuck or leaks, shut the main water valve instead and open a lower faucet to drain the line.

Plumbers find the cause by working from the outside in: handle, spout, cartridge or stem, then the supply stops and lines. Most faucet failures are small parts wearing out, not whole fixtures. The skill is knowing which small part it is before anything is taken apart.

What one drip costs you

The EPA says a faucet dripping once per second wastes more than 3,000 gallons a year. That is treated water you pay for and never use. A drip also stains porcelain and marks a part that is wearing out and will get worse.

A drip that stops when you turn the handle harder usually means a worn washer or seat. A drip that starts at the base of the spout points to O-rings in the body. Repair is almost always cheaper than waiting.

Signs the faucet needs more than a tighten

Tightening a nut fixes a few of these. Most need a part.

Handle leaks when you run water

Worn cartridge or O-rings. The faucet works but weeps around the base.

Hot and cold mix wrong

A worn cartridge lets the hot and cold lines blend, so the temperature wanders.

Weak flow from one faucet only

Mineral buildup in the aerator or a partly closed supply stop. The other fixtures tell you it is local.

Handle spins without control

A stripped handle screw or broken stem, which can leave the water running.

Pooling in the cabinet

A leaking supply line, a corroded stop valve or a tired drain connection.

Broken or stuck supply valves

The small stop valve under a sink is the part you rely on in an emergency, and it is the part most likely to be stuck. Many older valves have not been turned in years. When one finally gets moved, the stem packing can leak or the valve can seize half open.

A plumber replaces a bad stop with a new quarter-turn valve, which gives you a dependable shutoff before the next failure. If the stop is soldered into old copper or threaded onto galvanized pipe, the repair takes longer than a simple swap. For house-wide corroded pipe, ask about repiping an older house.

Shower and tub valve problems

Behind the wall, shower and tub valves use a cartridge or stem that wears just like a sink faucet. Signs are a drip from the tub spout after you shut off, a shower that turns scalding when another fixture runs, or a handle that no longer stops the water. Some valves can be repaired from the front, but others need an access hole in the wall, so the price is clear before work starts.

Old buildings, original fixtures

Mainstrasse Village is a 91-acre National Register district with Federal, Victorian, Italianate and Queen Anne buildings, and Covington's population peaked at 65,252 in 1930. Homes of that age often still carry original or early replacement supply stops and fixtures. Expect corroded stop valves and old shutoffs that were never replaced, which is why a plumber may suggest swapping the stops while the faucet is out.

If a faucet repair turns into relocating a sink or moving a supply line, the Kentucky Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction says moving fixtures needs a plumbing permit. Permit rules vary, so ask the Kentucky Division of Plumbing or the local plumbing inspector.
